Syllables must be exactly one vowel long (short or long)
Long vowels only appear in stressed syllables or elevated forms (-öö)

Syllable structure follows (C)(C)V(C
C = any consonant
V = vowel
Optional second C allows for only clusters that consist of these
- Voiceless + voiceless stop
	- pt
	- tp
	- kt, kp
- Stop + liquid/trill approximant
	- pl, pr, pj
	- bl, br, bj, bv
	- tl, tr, tj, tv
	- dl, dr, dv
	- kr, kv
- Nasal + stop
	- mp, mt, mk, mb, md
	- np, nt, nk, ng

Illegal CC clusters:
- Voiceless + voiceless stop → pk, tk
- Stop + liquid/trill approximant → pv, dj, kj, kl
- Nasal + stop → nb, nd, mg

No more than 2 consonants at the start
No more than 1 consonant at the end (except nasals)
Stress always the first syllable of the root
In compounds the stress shifts to first syllable of the entire word  
Long vowels are naturally stressed

A root is 1–2 syllables, and can not be broken down into smaller parts
A compound is a word that is made of two or more roots + affixes, can often be 3+ syllables

When a consonant cluster violates the root or compound syllable rules, insert a short vowel
(often /e/ or /ɤ/)

When compounding two independent nouns or titles & names, add a space in-between
